# The Notice

The lift in the east wing had been out of service for eleven days. On the
twelfth morning it was working again, and Ellen Marsh found this out by
pressing the button out of habit, the way she had pressed it every morning
since the notice went up, and hearing the doors open.

She did not get in. She stood and looked at the open lift for a moment. Then
she went to find someone to tell.

The person she found was Robert Ayles, who managed the building and who was
eating a sandwich at his desk with a stack of forms in front of him. He put
the sandwich down when she came in.

"The lift is working," Ellen said.

"Which lift."

"The east one. The one with the notice on it."

Robert looked at the forms. There were nine of them, and they were the forms
for the lift repair, and they had been filled in over eleven days by four
different people, and the last of them had not been signed.

"It can't be working," he said. "The engineer is coming on Thursday."

"It opened when I pressed the button."

"That's not the same as working."

Ellen said that she thought it was fairly close. Robert explained that a lift
was certified as working by an engineer, and that until an engineer had
certified it, the lift was out of service regardless of what it did when you
pressed the button. He said this was not his rule. He said it in the voice of
a man who had explained it before.

They went down together to look at it. The notice was still taped to the doors
at chest height, printed in the building's official typeface, and it said LIFT
OUT OF SERVICE — PLEASE USE STAIRS. The doors were open behind it. The light
inside was on.

Robert pressed the button. The doors, which were already open, closed. Then
they opened again.

"There," said Ellen.

"That proves nothing."

He stepped inside and stood in the middle of the car with his hands at his
sides, looking at the ceiling, and Ellen watched him from the corridor. The
doors closed. There was a pause of about four seconds. Then the lift went down
one floor and stopped, and the doors opened onto the basement, where Robert had
not asked to go and where nobody had pressed anything.

Ellen took the stairs. When she got to the basement Robert was still standing
inside the car, and the doors were still open, and he had not moved.

"It brought you here," she said.

"I noticed."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

They looked at the panel. There were seven buttons and none of them were lit.
Robert pressed the one for the ground floor. Nothing happened. He pressed it
again. The doors closed and the lift went up two floors, past the ground floor,
and opened on the first, where a woman with a box was waiting, and who got in
without looking at either of them, and pressed the button for the basement.

The lift went to the fourth floor and opened.

"Is this normal," the woman said.

"The lift is out of service," Robert said.

The woman looked at the open doors, and at the corridor beyond them, and at
the two people standing in the lift with her, and then she picked up her box
and got out and walked to the stairs without saying anything else.

Robert stayed in the lift for another twenty minutes. Ellen brought him a chair
from the fourth-floor waiting area, which he did not use, and a cup of tea,
which he drank standing up. Each time the doors closed the lift moved, and each
time it moved it went somewhere nobody had chosen, and each time it arrived the
doors opened and stayed open until they closed again on their own.

At eleven o'clock he came out and went back upstairs and got the ninth form
and signed it in the box marked ENGINEER, and then crossed that out, and wrote
his own name in the box marked REPORTED BY, and then put the form at the bottom
of the stack rather than the top.

"What did you write," Ellen asked.

"That the lift is out of service."

"It isn't, though."

"No," Robert said. "But that's the form there is."

He went back to his sandwich. The notice stayed up. For the next three days
the lift ran, and the people in the east wing used the stairs, because the
notice said to, and on Thursday the engineer came and found nothing wrong with
it and left, and the notice came down that afternoon, and after that the lift
stopped working again and stayed stopped for a month.
